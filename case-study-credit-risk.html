Designing a unified decision platform for NBFC credit teams

NBFC credit analysts process hundreds of applications daily, yet their workflow forces them to jump across 4–6 fragmented tools before they can make a single decision. The problem isn't missing data — it's that no system synthesizes it. This case study explores how a unified command center can transform credit operations.

Step 01 — Problem

Credit decisions are slow because the data lives everywhere but the analyst

NBFC credit analysts are asked to process hundreds of loan applications daily. But the data they need to make decisions is fragmented across completely disconnected systems — bureau data in one platform, bank statements in another, employer verification via email threads, and internal credit policy in a shared drive or a manager's head. The result is predictable: slow decisions, high cognitive effort, and inconsistent risk assessment across the team.

Step 03 — Research

What the research revealed

Transparency note: This is a product design project. Research is based on publicly available information, industry reports on NBFC underwriting workflows, and analysis of digital lending operations — not primary user interviews at a specific company.
Research approach: Analysis of NBFC underwriting workflows, RBI reports on digital lending, industry benchmarks from lending automation vendors, and secondary research on credit risk management practices in Indian financial institutions.
Finding 01
Decision Time Baseline
Industry average credit decision time for retail loans in the NBFC sector ranges from 2–4 hours for manual underwriting workflows, across application intake, data gathering, analysis, and decision documentation.
2–4 hours per application
Finding 02
Data Fragmentation is Universal
Credit analysts in manual underwriting workflows typically context-switch between 4–6 distinct tools or systems per application — bureau portals, bank statement analyzers, internal CRMs, email, spreadsheets, and document repositories.
4–6 tools per application
Finding 03
Consistency Gap in Borderline Cases
Manual underwriting without structured decision frameworks shows 15–20% decision variance for borderline applicants — the same file reviewed by two different analysts can yield different outcomes, creating regulatory and portfolio risk.
15–20% variance on borderline cases
Finding 04
Decision Support ROI is Proven
Lending automation and decision support tools — when successfully adopted — reduce analyst time-on-data-gathering by 40–60%, freeing cognitive capacity for higher-order judgment calls rather than information retrieval.
40–60% reduction in data time
Step 04 — Insight

The bottleneck is not data — it's synthesis

Analysts are not slow because they lack information. They're slow because they must manually gather, reconcile, and synthesize data from 4–6 sources before they can even begin the actual credit assessment. The decision-making moment — the part that requires human judgment — is buried under an hour of information archaeology.

01
Data exists → Synthesis is missing
Every data point the analyst needs already exists — CIBIL score, salary credits, employment tenure. The gap is that there's no single surface that brings it together in a decision-ready format. The tool isn't missing; the integration is.
02
Decisions are slow → Confidence is lacking
Analysts delay decisions not because they're uncertain about their judgment, but because they're never fully confident they've seen the complete picture. Slow decisions are a symptom of incomplete information visibility, not weak analysis skills.
03
Inconsistency comes from unstructured frameworks, not analyst skill
Variance in decisions across analysts isn't a talent problem — it's a product problem. Without a structured checklist or decision framework embedded in the workflow, each analyst builds their own mental model. The solution is structure, not training.
Step 05 — Opportunity

One screen. All signals. One decision.

A unified credit command center that aggregates all data sources and provides structured decision support — turning a 4-hour manual process into a 45-minute structured workflow. Not by automating the decision, but by eliminating the information retrieval problem so analysts can focus entirely on judgment.
NBFC
Market opportunity signal Indian NBFCs collectively process millions of loan applications monthly across retail, MSME, and personal loan segments. Even a 30% decision time reduction represents enormous operational leverage — fewer analysts needed per volume, faster approval turnaround creating competitive differentiation, and reduced cost-per-decision at scale.

Step 06 — Prioritization

What to build first

Given the insights, the prioritization question becomes: which features directly eliminate the synthesis bottleneck, and which are valuable-but-secondary? Using a RICE-informed framework — weighted toward Reach + Impact — the roadmap below emerges naturally.

Feature Impact Effort Priority
Unified data view (all sources on one screen) High Medium P0
Risk score visualization High Low P0
Structured decision checklist Medium Low P1
Decision audit trail Medium Medium P1
ML-based anomaly detection High High P2
Case notes & team collaboration Low Low P3
Framework applied: RICE — Reach (all analysts daily), Impact (direct bottleneck removal), Confidence (evidenced by research), Effort (engineering complexity). P0 items were chosen for high impact with manageable effort. ML features pushed to P2 due to data requirements and model development time.
Step 07 — Solution

Credit Risk Command Center

A single-pane B2B dashboard that aggregates bureau data, bank statement analysis, employment verification, and ML risk scoring into one structured interface for credit decisions. The goal: eliminate context-switching entirely, and embed structured decision support directly into the analyst's workflow.

01
01
Unified Applicant View
All data sources — bureau, bank statements, employment verification, application details — surfaced on a single screen. Zero tab-switching. The analyst never needs to leave the platform to build their understanding of the applicant.
02
02
Risk Signal Dashboard
Bureau score, bank health metrics, income stability trend, and employer verification status — all visualized in scannable, color-coded signal cards. Turns raw data into decision-ready signals that analysts can assess in seconds, not minutes.
03
03
Decision Checklist
A structured yes/no framework with mandatory fields that enforces consistent evaluation of all key risk factors before a decision can be submitted. Reduces decision variance by ensuring every analyst covers the same ground, every time.
04
04
Audit Trail
Every decision is logged with a complete data snapshot — what was seen, what was checked, what was decided, and why. Enables regulatory compliance, continuous model improvement, and performance benchmarking across analysts.

Step 09 — Metrics

How I would measure success

North Star Metric
Average Credit Decision Time per Application
Target: < 45 minutes (from 2–4 hour baseline) — without increasing decision error rate
Decision Time
< 45 min
Target per application, down from 2–4 hour baseline. Measured from application assignment to decision submission in the platform.
Decision Consistency
≥ 90%
Agreement rate on comparable cases reviewed by different analysts. Tested via shadow review panels on matched applicant profiles monthly.
Analyst Efficiency
+35% throughput
Applications processed per analyst per day. Leading indicator of whether the platform is creating real capacity vs. just shifting where time is spent.
Data Re-verification Rate
< 5%
% of decisions requiring a second pass due to data errors or missing information. A proxy for data aggregation quality within the platform.
Feature Adoption Rate
80% in 60 days
% of team actively using all P0 + P1 features within 60 days of rollout. If adoption lags, the product has a UX or onboarding problem, not a feature problem.
Decision Default Rate
No regression
Portfolio default rate on approved loans must not increase. Speed and consistency gains are meaningless if they come at the cost of credit quality. Guardrail metric.
Step 10 — Experiment

How I would validate this

A three-phase rollout designed to minimize risk while generating high-quality signal. Each phase answers a specific question before the next phase begins — and each has clear criteria for proceeding or pivoting.

01
Shadow Mode — Observe Without Changing Behavior
Week 1–2
Run the Credit Risk Command Center alongside the existing workflow — analysts continue their normal process, but all data is simultaneously aggregated in the new platform. Compare decisions made without the tool against what the tool's signals would suggest. This surfaces calibration gaps and data quality issues before any behavioral change is introduced. Key question: Does the platform surface the same data the analyst finds manually?
02
Controlled Pilot — Measure Real Behavior Change
Week 3–6
Route 50% of incoming applications through the new platform (treatment group) and 50% through the existing workflow (control group). Measure decision time, consistency scores, and analyst satisfaction for both cohorts. Applications should be matched by loan amount, applicant profile, and analyst experience level to control for confounding variables. Key question: Does the tool create statistically significant improvement in decision time without degrading decision quality?
03
Full Rollout — Scale with Confidence
Week 7+ (contingent on pilot results)
Based on pilot results meeting the success threshold, migrate 100% of applications to the platform. Continue monitoring North Star and guardrail metrics weekly for the first 90 days. Set up an analyst feedback loop (bi-weekly 15-min syncs) to capture friction points not visible in quantitative data. Begin planning P2 feature development (ML anomaly detection) based on accumulated decision data.
TARGET
Success threshold for proceeding from Pilot to Full Rollout: ≥ 20% reduction in average decision time for the treatment group with no statistically significant increase in the default rate on loans approved through the new platform within the first 60-day cohort.

PM Learnings from this Case Study
01
In B2B, the enemy is context-switching — every tool switch is not just a time cost, it's a cognitive cost. The most powerful product decision in this case study wasn't a feature; it was the choice to eliminate the need to leave the platform entirely.
02
Decision support is not automation — the goal was never to replace the analyst's judgment. It was to give analysts more signal, faster, so their judgment could operate at its best. Framing matters: this is an intelligence tool, not a replacement tool.
03
Consistency is a product problem, not a training problem — if decision variance exists across a trained team, the root cause is almost always an unstructured process, not individual skill. The solution is to embed structure into the workflow itself.
