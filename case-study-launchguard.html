Problem

Product launches fail for predictable reasons.
We just don't track them.

60–70%
of product launches fail to meet their initial success metrics — a consistent industry observation across B2B and B2C software. Most of these failures trace back to the same root causes, every time.
The Current Toolstack Gap
Notion Stores plans, can't analyze them
Jira Tracks tasks, not launch risk
Confluence Documents info, not risk signals
LaunchGuard AI Analyzes plans → surfaces risks

Research

What I found when
I talked to PMs.

Research methodology: Interviews with 5 product managers in my MBA network + analysis of publicly documented product launch failures + gap analysis of existing PM tooling. All direct quotes are paraphrased from interview notes.
01
From PM Interviews
"We usually discover a launch risk in the last week — never earlier." Every PM interviewed described a variation of this experience. Risk identification is reactive, not proactive.
02
From PM Interviews
"GTM and product are always misaligned on the launch date." 4 of 5 PMs mentioned cross-team alignment as their single biggest launch challenge. No tool enforces this alignment.
03
PM Tool Analysis
Existing tools — Jira, Notion, Confluence — store launch information but don't analyze it for risk. They're document repositories, not risk engines. The gap between "storing" and "synthesizing" is where launches fail.
04
Launch Failure Pattern Analysis
Most documented product failures — Google+, Amazon Fire Phone, and others — show risks that were visible in retrospect but untracked in real-time. The signals existed; there was no system to read them.
05
Market Gap
No dedicated AI-powered launch risk tool exists in the market as a standalone product. Risk management lives in checklists, spreadsheets, or the PM's own memory — none of which scale.
Core Market Gap
Zero dedicated AI-powered launch risk management products exist today. The space is completely open.
Insight

Most launch failures are not surprises —
they're unread signals.

The problem is not a lack of information — it's a lack of synthesis. Launch plans already contain the signals of future failure. Teams just don't have a structured way to read them systematically before it's too late.
Insight 01
Risk identification is not a skill gap — it's a tooling gap. PMs know how to think about risk. They just don't have a product that helps them do it systematically, at scale, across every launch.
Insight 02
Cross-team misalignment is the #1 root cause. Product knows the plan; GTM doesn't know what they need to prepare. No tool enforces or surfaces this gap proactively — until it's too late.
Insight 03
Post-launch learning is never systematized. Teams repeat the same launch mistakes because no one captures what went wrong in a retrievable, actionable format. Every team re-learns from scratch.
Opportunity

An AI layer
on top of your launch plan.

Product Opportunity
01
Connect to where launch plans live — Notion, Google Docs, Confluence
02
Analyze the plan using LLMs — structured document understanding at scale
03
Identify gaps and risks across 4 categories — ranked by severity
04
Surface structured action items with owner assignment per risk
Market Size: Every product team globally. Focused on mid-size tech companies (100–5000 employees), this is a large, underserved segment with budget and urgency.
Why Now
✓
LLMs have made document understanding tractable. GPT-4 and similar models can read unstructured launch plans and extract structured risk signals with high accuracy.
✓
Account-level PM tooling is underserved. Linear, Jira, and Notion serve task management. No one owns launch execution and risk as a dedicated product category.
✓
The integration APIs exist. Notion, Google Docs, and Confluence all have public APIs — LaunchGuard can plug in without building from scratch.
✓
PMs are AI-ready. Research shows PMs are early adopters of AI tooling and have budget authority for new tools that save time.

Solution

LaunchGuard AI:
Four core capabilities.

01
Capability 01
Launch Plan Analyzer
Connect your launch plan from Notion, Google Docs, or Confluence. LaunchGuard AI reads the document and identifies risk gaps across all four categories: Technical, GTM, Metric Definition, and Communication. No manual input required.
02
Capability 02
Risk Matrix
A visual grid of all identified risks, rated by likelihood and severity. Each risk includes a suggested mitigation action and an assignable owner — converting analysis into accountability in one step.
03
Capability 03
Team Readiness Checklist
Auto-generated checklists per team — PM, Engineering, Marketing, Support — with clear owners, due dates, and completion tracking. Every team knows exactly what they need to do before launch day.
04
Capability 04
Launch Monitoring
A day-of launch monitoring dashboard that tracks key metrics against pre-defined success thresholds. Know in real-time whether the launch is performing as expected — and get alerts when it isn't.

Metrics

How we measure
if LaunchGuard works.

North Star Metric
Launch Success Rate
% of product launches by LaunchGuard users that meet their pre-defined D30 success metrics — the ultimate outcome we exist to improve.
01
Risks Identified per Plan
Average number of risks surfaced per launch plan analyzed. Validates that the AI is doing useful work.
02
% Risks Mitigated Before Launch
Of identified risks, what % were resolved before launch day. Measures whether LaunchGuard drives action, not just awareness.
03
Time to Risk-Ready State
Time from plan upload to complete risk matrix. Target: under 3 minutes. Tracks AI performance and UX friction.
04
Checklist Completion Rate
% of team readiness checklist items completed before launch. Cross-team alignment proxy — if checklist is ignored, we haven't changed behavior.
05
Post-Launch Incident Rate
Issues surfaced after launch that were not caught pre-launch. Declining over time = LaunchGuard is working.
06
PM NPS
Net Promoter Score from PM users. Bottom-up SaaS spreads when individual PMs love the product and share it with their teams.
Experiment

3-phase beta plan
to validate the core hypothesis.

1
Phase 1 · Closed Beta
Validate AI Accuracy with 10 PM Teams
Recruit 10 product managers from MBA and professional network. Each PM uploads a real launch plan and rates each identified risk as valid / not valid / missed risk. This directly measures whether the AI output is trustworthy enough to act on.
Measure: Risk identification accuracy (target: 80%+ valid), time saved vs. manual review (target: 3x faster), qualitative feedback on missing categories.
2
Phase 2 · Open Beta
Measure Adoption, Retention, and Conversion
Open beta to 50 product teams. Focus shifts from accuracy validation to product-market fit signals. Track weekly active usage, plan upload frequency, and conversion from free to paid beta.
Measure: Week-4 retention, plan uploads per user per month, NPS score, conversion rate to paid subscription intent.
3
Phase 3 · PMF Gate
Sean Ellis Test — Product-Market Fit Threshold
At end of open beta, survey all active users: "How would you feel if you could no longer use LaunchGuard?" Gate to full launch: 40%+ responding "Very Disappointed." This is the Sean Ellis test — the industry-standard PMF benchmark for early-stage SaaS.
PMF Threshold: ≥40% "Very Disappointed" response. Below threshold → iterate on core value prop before commercial launch.
PMF
Why the Sean Ellis test? It's the most reliable leading indicator of PMF in B2B SaaS. It doesn't measure satisfaction — it measures dependency. If users would be "very disappointed" without your product, you've built something that changes how they work. That's the standard LaunchGuard needs to hit.

PM Learnings
01
The best AI products don't replace human judgment — they structure it. LaunchGuard doesn't decide what is or isn't a risk. It surfaces candidates. The PM still makes the call. This distinction is what makes AI products trustworthy vs. threatening to the user.
02
Distribution matters as much as product in PM tooling. PM tools spread bottom-up — one PM tries it, loves it, shares with the team. This means the first 10 users are everything. Get the product right enough that one PM would genuinely recommend it to a colleague without being asked.
03
The hardest part of building AI products is not the AI — it's defining what "correct" output looks like. Prompt engineering, output validation, and edge case handling require the PM to be extremely precise about the problem definition. Vague problems produce vague AI output.
